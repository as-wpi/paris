The EWMA sizer's half-life of about eleven days is simply longer than these crashes. The calm-control episodes show the free component of any lookahead, 11–38% at k=5 (median about 20%), so the crash-specific reachable share is roughly the excess, some 20 points. By the pre-registered rule the verdict is **MIXED**: neither component captures two thirds of the joint bound, so approaches C–G all proceed in the registered order (two-speed sizer, shock rule, circuit breaker, asymmetric penalty, turbulence exit), then H.

**Test B — execution timing is not a lever.** Trading at the next open instead of the same close costs nothing (ΔSharpe +0.002, fast-crash MaxDD +0.1 pt, turnover ×0.97). A full extra day of lag (decide at close T−1, trade at close T) costs 0.023 Sharpe (CI [−0.045, −0.008]) and 0.1 pt of fast-crash MaxDD — below both bars — while the one-day lookahead ceiling from test A is worth +0.159 Sharpe and 1.5 pts. The asymmetry is the finding: information one day *earlier* is valuable, execution one day *later* is nearly free, because the strategy's positions change 1.7 times a year and the vol-target rebalances on band exits, not daily. Deployment convention stays as is; the next-open route is acceptable when the MOC route is unavailable. (The t+2 arm's turnover ×2.5 comes from re-deciding daily against a stale held weight — an artefact of the delayed loop as implemented, reported for honesty; it does not change the verdict.)
